# DATA.md: data coverage, quality findings and cleaning decisions

## 1. Dataset

**REFIT Electrical Load Measurements** (University of Strathclyde; Murray et al., 2015/2017), 20 UK
households in the Loughborough area (House 1 to 21, no House 14), 2013 to 2015. Each house has one
whole-house current clamp ("Aggregate") and nine plug-in Individual Appliance Monitors (IAMs,
`Appliance1` to `Appliance9`). Only **active power in watts** is recorded. The readme of the clean
release is saved in [`docs/CLEAN_READ_ME_081116.txt`](docs/CLEAN_READ_ME_081116.txt).

| Use in this repo | Files | Houses | Period used | Sampling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Section 1, cleaning | `REFIT_RAW_081116/RAW_House1_Part1.csv`, `RAW_House1_Part2.csv` | 1 | 2013-10-09 to 2015-07-10 (all) | irregular, median 7 s |
| Sections 2 and 3, EDA and model | `CLEAN_REFIT_081116/CLEAN_House{2,5,7,9,15,17}.csv` | 2, 5, 7, 9, 15, 17 | full span of each file (table in RESULTS.md) | ~8 s, resampled to 1 min |

Both archives are downloaded from the
[dataset page](https://pureportal.strath.ac.uk/en/datasets/refit-electrical-load-measurements/)
and extracted to `~/Downloads` (paths are set in `src/config.py`). The data is not committed.

**Limitations stated by the dataset itself.** IAMs were not synchronised (each may have updated at
any point in the last 6 to 8 s); data was written only when a load changed; occupants may have moved
plugs between appliances; "Television/Computer site" channels can hold several devices; there are
outages (notably February 2014). Whole-house readings include everything that is *not* monitored,
for example electric showers, immersion heaters and lighting.

## 2. Raw House 1: what we found

Full numbers are generated by `src/clean_house1.py` into
[`outputs/house1_quality_report.md`](outputs/house1_quality_report.md).

| Check | Finding | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| **Parsing** | 8,518,763 rows; every `Time` string parses; `Time` and `Unix` always agree within 2 s. | Use `Unix` as the timestamp. |
| **Time zone** | `Time == Unix` everywhere, and on 27 Oct 2013 the clock jumps **back** from 01:59:59 to 01:00:01, while on 30 Mar 2014 and 29 Mar 2015 the 01:00 to 02:00 hour is **missing**. So the logger stored **UK local clock time**, not UTC. | Convert to true UTC. In the autumn, rows of the repeated hour before the backward jump are BST and those after are GMT. |
| **Two files** | Part1 covers 2013-10-09 to 2014-10-01, Part2 covers 2014-06-27 to 2015-07-10: they **overlap by ~3 months** (945,403 Part2 rows). Over the overlap the two files agree to ~57 W per minute on average but Part2 has fewer rows. | Keep Part1 for the overlap; use Part2 only after Part1 ends. |
| **Row format differs** | Part1 has no blanks; Part2 has 5.3 M blank values because it writes one row per sensor report with the other channels blank (readme: Part2 sets unavailable sensors to NaN, Part1 to 0). | Rows sharing a timestamp are merged, keeping the last non-blank value per channel. Note Part1 zeros may hide "unavailable" periods. |
| **Ordering** | 946,002 rows are earlier than a preceding row, all explained by the DST repeat and the Part1/Part2 overlap. | Stable sort on UTC time. |
| **Duplicates** | 595,940 exact duplicate rows; 1,046,136 rows share a `Unix` second but differ in values. | Drop exact duplicates; for the same second keep the last non-blank value per channel (the most recent state). |
| **Repeated hour in Part2** | Around 26 Oct 2014 the repeated hour has *no* backward jump: the rows were already interleaved, so BST and GMT readings cannot be told apart. | Drop those 986 rows; the hour becomes a flagged outage. |
| **Impossible values** | No negatives. Maximum values such as 32,767, 65,535, 73,725 and 98,301 W are sensor error codes (powers of two minus one or bit patterns). 3,949 appliance readings exceed 4 kW; 127 aggregate readings exceed 20 kW. | Appliance > 4,000 W set to missing (a 13 A UK plug cannot exceed ~3.1 kW; same 4 kW rule as the official cleaning). Aggregate > 20,000 W set to missing (above a typical 80 to 100 A domestic supply and far outside the observed distribution). Aggregate readings of 10 to 11 kW are **kept**: there are thousands of them, consistent with an electric shower. |
| **Irregular sampling** | Interval between rows: median 7 s, 99th percentile 15 s; 715 intervals over 2 min, 33 over 1 h, the longest 41.6 days. | Resample to 1 minute (mean of the readings in each minute). |
| **Outages** | 122,187 of 920,031 minutes (13.3%) have no reading at all; the longest outage runs from 24 Jan to 6 Mar 2014 (998 h). | See gap rule below. |

## 3. Cleaning pipeline (`src/clean_house1.py`)

1. Read Part1 and Part2; trim Part2 to after Part1's last timestamp.
2. Convert local clock time to UTC (DST rule above); drop the unresolvable repeated hour.
3. Sort by UTC time; drop exact duplicates; merge rows sharing a timestamp (last non-blank per channel).
4. Set impossible values to missing (appliance > 4 kW, aggregate > 20 kW).
5. Resample to 1-minute means; record the number of readings per minute.
6. **Gaps of 5 minutes or less** are filled by linear interpolation (7,061 minutes). At 1-minute
   resolution this mainly bridges missed polls; it cannot invent a whole appliance cycle.
   **Longer gaps are left empty and flagged** (`outage = True`, 121,393 minutes, 410 outages),
   listed in `outputs/house1_outages.csv`. Filling them would invent consumption we did not observe.

Output: `outputs/house1_1min.parquet` with the 10 power channels plus `n_samples`, `filled` and `outage`.

Plots (`figures/`): `house1_before_after_day.png` (aggregate, washing machine and fridge on a full
day), `house1_before_after_spike.png` (an 8 kW fridge spike removed), `house1_before_after_gap.png`
(a 25-hour outage that the raw plot draws as a straight line, flagged instead after cleaning) and
`house1_coverage.png` (daily share of minutes with data).

## 4. Clean data used for Sections 2 and 3 (`src/clean_data.py`)

The official clean files are already DST-corrected (UTC `Unix`), forward-filled between sensor
updates, and have IAM spikes > 4 kW replaced by 0. They add an `Issues` column (1 when the sum of
the IAMs exceeds the aggregate). Our extra steps: 1-minute means; the same 5-minute gap rule; the
`Issues` share per minute is kept for reporting. Houses and the washing-machine channel come from the
readme appliance list: H2 `Appliance2`, H5 `Appliance3`, H7 `Appliance5`, H9 `Appliance3`,
H15 `Appliance3`, H17 `Appliance4`. Houses 4 (two washing machines) and 13 (machine replaced in
March 2015) were left out.

## 5. Remaining concerns

- **Forward-filling in the clean data** means a sensor that stopped reporting keeps its last value
  until the next reading, so short dropouts look like steady load rather than gaps.
- **Part1 zeros vs Part2 blanks**: in Part1 an unavailable sensor reads 0, which is indistinguishable
  from "appliance off".
- **IAM issues**: 1.6 to 8.4% of minutes are flagged by REFIT because the appliances sum to more
  than the aggregate (unsynchronised sensors); we report but do not drop them.
- **Standby power**: House 5's washing machine draws a few watts most of the time, so "exactly 0 W"
  is a poor off-state definition; we use a 20 W threshold instead.
- **Appliance swaps** noted in the readme (and any not noted) can change a channel's meaning.
- The 4 kW and 20 kW thresholds are physical sanity limits, not a full spike filter: a fault that
  produces a plausible value (for example 2.5 kW on a fridge) is not caught.
